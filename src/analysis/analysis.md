# Publication analysis: non-unital memory noise in repeater protocol design

Research log, started 25 October 2026. This is a working document: numerical claims below are labeled by their validation status.

## Publication target and scope

The target is the [npj Quantum Information collection *Towards Large-scale Quantum Networks*](https://www.nature.com/collections/aahafehfad), which lists a submission deadline of **22 December 2026** and explicitly includes repeater protocols, quantum memories, and network simulation. The manuscript should therefore lead with a decision-relevant networking result, then use the state representation and efficient simulator as the enabling method.

## What the three source projects already establish

1. [Li, Coopmans & Elkouss, *Efficient optimization of cut-offs in quantum repeater chains*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04946) computes finite-time waiting distributions and Werner fidelity with cutoffs, then optimizes cutoffs for secret-key rate. Its main numerical examples use nested homogeneous chains. Its result that optimized cutoffs can rescue a zero-key regime is prior art.
2. [La Corte et al., *Bayesian Optimization for Repeater Protocols*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02208) extends the simulation to arbitrary node counts, heterogeneous hardware, and optimization over swap/distillation trees. In its results, balanced trees tend to win for homogeneous hardware; link-level distillation is useful in low-quality regimes, while heterogeneity changes preferred swap order. That paper explicitly did not optimize cutoffs jointly with trees.
3. The local `lilla-overleaf/main.tex` notes derive Bell-diagonal/Pauli-Fourier representations and a six-coordinate real-X subspace, with local Pauli noise plus amplitude damping, corrected or selected Bell outcomes, and recurrence distillation. The representation is **not a representation of arbitrary two-qubit states**. It is an invariant family for the specified state preparation and operations. Any claim of “general states” must state this limit. The implemented real-X coordinates are `(II, IZ, ZI, ZZ, XX, YY)`; local `Z` biases are the information absent in Bell-diagonal and Werner models.

## Current research thesis

The best candidate contribution is **decision error caused by non-unital noise approximation**: hold hardware, candidate protocols, and initial states fixed; optimize under physical amplitude damping plus dephasing and under an exactly defined approximation; execute both selected policies under the physical model; report absolute/relative rate regret, policy changes, and controls. A predicted-fidelity gap alone is insufficient. The current code supports the *local Pauli-channel twirl* baseline directly (`prediction_model=local_channel_twirl`), with the same contraction factors but zero affine translation. It does not yet support a complete Bell-state projection schedule, so those comparisons must not be claimed as run results.

Useful algebraic mechanism: a local non-unital channel has `Z -> q Z + h I` in the Heisenberg moment update; for an unnormalized real-X link, `ZZ' = h_L h_R II + h_L q_R IZ + q_L h_R ZI + q_L q_R ZZ`. The two local biases can feed into the future `ZZ` correlation and thus into the BB84 bit error. This makes six real coordinates sufficient for the modeled operations while four Bell populations can be insufficient across multiple waiting intervals. With all corrected swap outcomes and no further non-unital evolution, Bell projection retains final Bell populations; that is a required zero-discrepancy control.

There is a second, more direct mechanism for **outcome-selective swapping**. For normalized input links, the implemented accepted probability of the two `Φ` outcomes is `(1 + z_R(first) z_L(second))/2`, while for the two `Ψ` outcomes it is `(1 - z_R(first) z_L(second))/2`. A local Pauli-channel twirl sets both biases to zero and predicts equal acceptance probability `1/2` for either pair. Real amplitude damping of both links generally makes their local `Z` biases positive, so `Φ` and `Ψ` acceptance times can separate even if their final Bell populations are similar. This is analytically testable, and is distinct from final-state fidelity prediction. Outcome-conditioned processing and postselection are also in Davies et al.; a publication claim needs the **rate-aware protocol selection under timed memory noise**, not merely the probability formula.

## Literature and novelty boundaries (initial pass)

- [Davies, Avis & Wehner, *On the accuracy of twirled approximations in repeater chains*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16689) proves exact preservation of final Bell populations for their non-postselected swap-and-correct protocols when Bell-state-twirled inputs replace full states. Their theorem is a key control, not an obstacle: it does not cover arbitrary intervening amplitude-damping memory intervals, recurrence distillation, or postselected decisions. It also distinguishes state twirling from Werner approximation.
- [Mondal et al., *Efficient formulation of quantum network under amplitude damping noise*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18064) compare fixed repeater policies under amplitude damping and its Pauli-channel twirl, including fidelity and concurrence. An optimized physical rate/regret study must go beyond this fixed-policy comparison.
- [Goodenough, Coopmans & Towsley, *On noise in swap ASAP repeater chains*](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2025-05-15-1744/) derive analytic distribution and moments under a simpler swap-ASAP model, including a global cutoff. We should compare or validate an appropriate limit, and not claim first distribution-level treatment of memory noise.
- [Haldar et al., *Reducing classical communication costs in multiplexed quantum repeaters using hardware-aware quasi-local policies*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13168) study when to distill before or after swapping in hardware-aware policies. A new result must identify the added role of non-unital memory noise, not merely rediscover a distillation ordering.
- [Abdelhadi et al., *Adaptive Channel Reshaping for Improved Entanglement Distillation*](https://journals.aps.org/prresearch/abstract/10.1103/8kdf-37gp) design improved distillation for amplitude-damping channels. Our recurrence map is a restricted primitive; no general optimality claim for distillation is warranted.
- [Labay Mora, Ferreira da Silva & Wehner, *Reducing hardware requirements for entanglement distribution via joint hardware-protocol optimization*](https://doi.org/10.1088/2058-9565/ad57e9) already optimize generation, purification, and swap policies in a simulation with T1/T2 memory noise. We must not claim the first protocol optimization with amplitude damping. The differentiator is a controlled *approximation-induced decision loss* analysis, with matched physical and twirled channels and complete waiting-time distributions.
- [Haldar et al., *Fast and reliable entanglement distribution with quantum repeaters*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevApplied.21.024041) use reinforcement learning for homogeneous/inhomogeneous chains with imperfect hardware and short-coherence memories. This bounds any broad claim of being first to optimize swap timing or cutoffs jointly.
- [Murta et al., *Key rates for quantum key distribution protocols with asymmetric noise*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.101.062321) already show that asymmetry of bit and phase errors matters for QKD. A fidelity-matched Werner comparison should establish a specific repeater-policy effect, not just repeat that key fraction depends on error composition.
- [Brand, Coopmans & Elkouss, *Efficient computation of the waiting time and fidelity in quantum repeater chains*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07688) provide efficient baseline algorithms for waiting-time distributions. The new simulator should be framed as a state/noise-family extension of that lineage, not a first efficient waiting-time algorithm.
- [Goodenough, Elkouss & Wehner, *Optimising repeater schemes for the quantum internet*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12221) optimize repeater schemes over hardware-aware choices. Our contribution must be the controlled state-information and non-unital-noise decision analysis, rather than general optimization of repeaters.
- [Mendonça, Marchiolli & Galetti, *Entanglement universality of two-qubit X-states*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.3021) establish that X states are a known family. Their entanglement-equivalent X counterpart result does **not** imply arbitrary two-qubit repeater dynamics remain in the real-X subspace; our closure claim rests on the specific channel and operations, and should not be called a universal state representation.
- [Xing & Zhang, *Entanglement swapping through the amplitude damping noise channel*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12963363/) analyze a specific amplitude-damped swapping setup. They reinforce that amplitude damping in swaps is prior art; our target is the resulting timed-protocol design and model-induced policy loss.
- [*Disti-Mator, an entanglement distillation-based state estimator*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-02352-2) shows a practical route to estimating Bell-diagonal state parameters from purification statistics. This is relevant when motivating why a protocol optimizer might have more input information than a single fidelity number, although our simulations assume the state is already characterized.

## Candidate formal result: an exact sufficiency boundary

Let `P` set the two local real-X moments `IZ` and `ZI` to zero and preserve `(II, ZZ, XX, YY)`. Inspection of the implemented unnormalized maps gives `P S(x,y)=S(Px,Py)` for **all-corrected swapping** `S`, and `P D(x,y)=D(Px,Py)` for the specific recurrence distillation `D`; their traces also agree. For local axis-diagonal **unital** Pauli noise `N`, `PN=NP`. Therefore, by induction over a protocol tree and its retry renewals, starting from Bell-diagonal inputs, every time-resolved Bell population, accepted probability, and pooled BB84 objective can be evaluated in the four-coordinate Bell-diagonal representation for these operations, even with time cutoffs and heterogeneous Pauli rates. The induction uses linearity of the unnormalized weighted states and equality of each operation's success trace. This statement is narrower than a claim about arbitrary gates, state-dependent cutoffs, or full output states.

Two explicit failure modes delimit the theorem. With damping translation `h != 0`, `P N` and `N P` generally differ because `ZZ'` contains `h_L q_R IZ + q_L h_R ZI`; repeated waiting can make those discarded terms observable. With outcome-selective swapping, even the branch trace contains `± z_R(first) z_L(second)/2`, so `P S_selected` and `S_selected(Px,Py)` generally differ immediately. This motivates the **six-coordinate closure for the modeled channel and operations**, without a universal minimality claim. The all-corrected part is consistent with Davies et al.'s more general swapping theorem; the useful extension here is the exact criterion in the timed-memory/distillation simulator and its failure under non-unital evolution.

The efficient-join argument in `lilla-overleaf/main.tex` further shows why six coordinates do not require a quadratic time-pair sum: amplitude-damping translations are finite sums of exponentials, and the bilinear swap/distillation maps preserve finite separability in the two readiness times. Prefix sums evaluate single attempts in `O(T)` for fixed state dimension; Fourier renewal gives `O(T log T)` per protocol unit. This is a derivation for the specified sequential Pauli-plus-damping channel and memory-time cutoffs, not a benchmark of observed runtime. Its result should be checked against the dense oracle and timing measurements before a speed claim.

## Preliminary results: exact channel mechanism and first enumeration

**Algebraic control (checked against `accepted_bell_swap`):** start from two perfect `Φ+` links, and apply amplitude damping with survival `η=0.8` to both qubits of each link. Each link has real-X coordinates `(1, 0.2, 0.2, 0.68, 0.8, -0.8)`. The physical probability of accepting the `Φ` pair of Bell outcomes is **0.52**, and for the `Ψ` pair it is **0.48**. Projecting the links to Bell-diagonal states before the measurement gives **0.50** for each. The conditioned output `ZZ` correlators are **0.4830769** (`Φ`) and **0.44** (`Ψ`) physically, against **0.4624** for either projected branch. With all corrected outcomes, the two models both give probability **1** and `ZZ=0.4624`. This is a transparent, exact zero-discrepancy control alongside a nonzero postselection effect. It is not an optimized chain result.

**Exploratory chain enumeration:** `python -m src.analysis.protocol_regret_scan` enumerates 42 candidate policies (two ordered three-link trees, seven shared cutoffs, three global Bell-outcome policies) in each of 24 homogeneous scenarios. It stores all candidate predictions and coverage in `src/analysis/protocol_regret_results.json`. One near-threshold point, `p_gen=0.2`, `F0=0.99`, `p_swap=0.9`, pure relaxation `T1=8`, `T2=16` slots, has physical maximum **6.57227e-5 bits/slot** at cutoff 1 and all-corrected outcomes. The matched local-channel-twirl model predicts **zero key for every candidate**, so its argmax is entirely tied. Its first enumerated candidate has no cutoff and zero physical rate; choosing it would lose the full 6.57227e-5 bits/slot. **This is a model false-negative threshold, not robust optimizer regret**, because a twirled-model tie can also select the physical winner. The cutoff scan is being refined and must be checked at doubled horizon.

The other 23 initial scenarios gave no material model-selection regret within this candidate set; many harsh points had zero physical key as well. At `p_gen=0.2`, `F0=0.99`, pure relaxation and `T1>=20`, physical and twirled models chose the same cutoff. This negative result argues against publishing a broad claim that channel twirling *usually* changes optimal cutoff. We are therefore testing heterogeneous generation and distillation placement before settling the empirical thesis.

## Stronger preliminary result: fidelity-matched Werner optimization can select harmful distillation

The controlled scan in `src/analysis/distillation_family_scan.py` enumerates 35 two-link policies: shared cutoff `None, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32` slots, crossed with no recurrence, recurrence at the left leaf, right leaf, both leaves, or the root after swapping. All use all-corrected Bell outcomes. Every comparison keeps `p_gen=0.2`, `p_swap=0.9`, pure relaxation (`T2=2T1`), the same hardware success parameters, and exactly the same `Φ+` input fidelity. The Werner-input proxy changes **only the initial-state family**, not the physical memory channel; it then propagates the full real-X state without re-twirling after operations. This isolates information lost when only input fidelity is known. All 35 candidates in the central cases have coverage above 0.999 at horizon 2048. `src/analysis/distillation_family_results.json` contains every candidate's state-specific prediction and status.

| True initial state | `F0` | `T1` slots | True best (cutoff, recurrence) | Werner-selected policy | True best rate | Werner policy's true rate | True rate loss |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Phase error: `F Φ+ + (1-F) Φ−` | 0.95 | 30 | (1, none) | (4, root) | 0.02711096 | 0.01102915 | **59.3%** |
| Phase error | 0.95 | 80 | (4, none) | (16, root) | 0.03884159 | 0.02031111 | **47.7%** |
| Phase error | 0.97 | 80 | (4, none) | (16, root) | 0.05186578 | 0.03201698 | **38.3%** |
| Bit error: `F Φ+ + (1-F) Ψ+` | 0.95 | 30 | (2, none) | (4, root) | 0.03008066 | 0.02153549 | **28.4%** |

These are **exact finite-candidate policy-transfer losses**, not global-optimality statements over arbitrary repeater controls. The baseline predicts a benefit from root recurrence on Werner links; on phase-biased links, this particular bilateral-CNOT recurrence often consumes time while increasing the effective phase-error burden. The result identifies a concrete way that discarding the error composition changes a protocol decision, beyond merely changing its predicted fidelity. It is consistent with prior knowledge that QKD is sensitive to asymmetric error composition (Murta et al.); the proposed new contribution is the quantified, waiting-time-normalized *distillation and cutoff decision loss* over matched physical scenarios.

**Validation completed for the central case.** At horizons 2048, 4096, and 8192, the physical optimum's rate is respectively `0.0271109631438`, `0.0271109631430`, and `0.0271109631413` bits/slot; the Werner-selected policy's physical rate is `0.0110291480118`, `0.0110291480107`, and `0.0110291480082`. All four physical/baseline evaluations have coverage above `0.999999999998` at the smallest horizon. This makes the loss stable to the tested finite-time truncation. The physical optimum has `(eX,eZ)=(0.10317,0.01951)` and mean wait `14.10` slots. The Werner-selected root recurrence on the true phase-error state gives `(0.18128,0.00700)` and mean wait `23.30` slots: it suppresses bit errors but raises phase errors and spends substantially more time. The Werner model incorrectly predicts that same recurrence improves its own rate from `0.01524263` to `0.01564587` bits/slot. These numbers are from the shared simulator, with no Monte Carlo uncertainty; model and hardware assumptions remain the main limitation.

**Lower-generation-probability check:** `src/analysis/generation_scale_case.py` repeats the phase-error comparison at `p_gen=0.02`, `F0=0.95`, `T1=300`, `T2=600` slots, `p_swap=0.9`, with cutoffs scaled to `None, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320`. At horizon 32768 all **35/35** candidates are covered. The true optimum uses cutoff 20 with no distillation, yielding `0.0022989192` bits/slot. The Werner-input proxy selects cutoff 40 with root distillation, which yields `0.0008799214` physically: **61.7% rate loss**. The effect therefore persists at one order of magnitude lower link-generation success probability when the memory and cutoffs scale correspondingly. This remains a dimensionless scenario; no claim about a particular hardware platform or seconds is made.

**Noise controls** in `src/analysis/noise_control_scan.py` / `src/analysis/noise_control_results.json` use the same 35 policies and phase-biased `F0=0.95` input. With no memory noise, the physical optimum has `0.06817177` bits/slot and no distillation, whereas the Werner proxy chooses distillation at both leaves; its policy yields `0.03091293` on the true state (54.7% loss). With pure dephasing (`T2=60` slots, no damping), both choose no distillation but different cutoffs, causing only 2.85% rate loss. With pure relaxation (`T1=30`, `T2=60`), the model switches to harmful root distillation and loses 59.3%. **Thus the large loss is fundamentally about error composition under a fixed recurrence primitive; amplitude damping is one relevant regime, not the sole cause.** A manuscript must not attribute the entire effect to non-unitality.

The current recurrence choice is restricted: local basis rotations or a different distillation protocol could process phase-biased errors more effectively. The claim must specify the supported operation set, and a stronger paper should add at least one basis-adapted distillation comparator or explain hardware constraints that fix the implemented bilateral-CNOT recurrence. The result is nonetheless a genuine optimization error for the candidate operation set, since the physical and Werner searches use identical actions and hardware.

The recurrence mechanism is explicit. For a phase-only Bell mixture with `w=2F-1`, the input coordinates are `(1,0,0,1,w,-w)`. Applying the repository's equal-outcome bilateral-CNOT recurrence to two identical copies succeeds with probability **1**, but gives `(1,0,0,1,w²,-w²)`. Its phase error rises from `(1-w)/2=1-F` to `(1-w²)/2=2F(1-F)`. At `F=0.95`, this is `0.05 -> 0.095` even before additional waiting. The Werner input has a different `ZZ` error composition and the same recurrence can improve its pooled key fraction. This exact calculation explains the direction of the observed optimizer error; it also shows why an adaptable distillation basis is a key follow-up.

There is an elementary **fidelity-only impossibility witness** for this fixed recurrence map. A Werner state with the same `F` has `u=(4F-1)/3` and coordinates `(1,0,0,u,u,-u)`. Recurring two identical copies gives conditional Bell fidelity `F'_W=(1+2u+5u²)/(4(1+u²))`, so `F'_W-F = u(1-u)(3u-1)/(4(1+u²)) > 0` for `1/2<F<1`. For the phase-only state, `F'phase=F²+(1-F)²<F` in the same interval. Thus two valid inputs with the same `F` have opposite fidelity response to the identical physical operation. A one-number input fidelity cannot predict this recurrence response uniformly. This is a simple analytic lemma, not a claim that X states or recurrence protocols themselves are new.
At `F=0.95`, the code's direct operation map gives `F'phase=0.905` with success probability `1`, versus `F'_W=0.96496437` with success probability `0.93555556`, confirming the symbolic calculation.

**Three-link cutoff-only transfer:** `src/analysis/state_family_scan.py` enumerates 42 policies (two ordered trees, seven cutoffs, three Bell-outcome policies) at fixed `p_gen=0.2`, `p_swap=0.9`, pure relaxation `T1=50`, `T2=100`, and `F0=0.95`. If the true input is the bit-error mixture `0.95 Φ+ + 0.05 Ψ+`, both physically optimal trees use cutoff **2** and yield `0.00910436` bits/slot. A fidelity-matched Werner input model selects cutoff **1** and yields `0.00784888` when run on the true state: **13.8% loss**. All 42 candidates have coverage above 0.999. Repeating the selected candidates at horizons 2048 and 4096 changes these rates by less than `1.3e-12` bits/slot. Here the only changed action is the cutoff; distillation is absent. The Werner model predicts zero key at cutoff 2, while the true bit-error state tolerates it and benefits from reducing mean waiting time from `36.63` to `22.63` slots. This is a stronger network-level check that the result is not solely a distillation-primitive artifact.

**Four-link extension:** `src/analysis/four_link_family_scan.py` crosses the five ordered four-link swap trees, six shared cutoffs, and no/root/all-leaf recurrence placement: 90 policies per scenario, with `p_gen=0.2`, `p_swap=0.9`, `T1=80`, `T2=160` and all-corrected outcomes. For `F0=0.95` phase-error input, the physical optimum is the balanced tree, cutoff 1, no distillation at `0.00188007` bits/slot. The Werner model selects the balanced tree, cutoff 4, root recurrence; that policy yields `0.00086146` on the true state, **54.2% lower**. At `F0=0.98`, the corresponding loss is **10.7%** (`0.00784360` vs `0.00700057` bits/slot). At horizon 4096, only 76/90 and 80/90 candidates respectively passed the 0.999 coverage test. For the central `F0=0.95` case, increasing the horizon to **32768** covers **all 90/90 candidates** and leaves both winning policies unchanged: rates `0.0018800655528` and `0.0008614642721` bits/slot. The complete 90-policy result is in `src/analysis/four_link_phase_32768.json`; the `F0=0.98` result remains a covered-subset comparison until its slow candidates are resolved.

## Non-unital noise changes the estimated memory threshold

For homogeneous three-link Werner inputs with `F0=0.99`, `p_gen=0.2`, `p_swap=0.9`, pure relaxation `T2=2T1`, all-corrected swaps, no distillation, and one fixed tree, `src/analysis/key_threshold.py` evaluates nine shared cutoffs and locates the signed BB84 fraction's zero crossing for cutoff 1. The physical real-X model crosses at **`T1=7.95595` slots**; a matched Pauli-channel-twirled memory model crosses at **`T1=8.34896` slots**, a **0.393-slot / 4.94%** stricter predicted memory requirement. Across the checked probes from `T1=7.5` to `9`, cutoff 1 maximized the signed fraction among the nine cutoffs in both models. At `T1=8`, the physical optimum yields `6.57227e-5` bits/slot while every twirled candidate yields zero. This is a concrete hardware-threshold prediction error, complementary to the fidelity-matched Werner policy-transfer losses above. The root precision is numerical at horizon 1024; selected-rate stability was checked at 2048 and 4096. The result is limited to the stated hardware, state, candidate controls, and asymptotic pooled BB84 formula.

## Manuscript direction for the collection

Working title: **State information changes quantum-repeater control: efficient simulation under anisotropic and non-unital memory noise**.

The central story should have three connected claims:

1. **Method:** within the specified family of independent local axis-diagonal Pauli noise plus amplitude damping, real-X input states, corrected/selected Bell swaps, one recurrence primitive, heterogeneous node rates, and memory-time cutoffs, six unnormalized Pauli coordinates close under the operations. Their success-weighted waiting distributions retain the finite exponential factorization, enabling efficient deterministic evaluation. This is more general than Werner or Bell-diagonal simulation, and cheaper than propagating a generic 4×4 density matrix, but does not cover arbitrary two-qubit states/channels.
2. **Theory:** identify the precise reduction boundary. Bell populations are sufficient under the supported unital/all-corrected operation set; local affine translations and postselection expose the discarded biases. A single fidelity cannot even predict the *sign* of this recurrence's fidelity change for fidelity-matched Werner versus phase-only inputs. These statements are exact algebraic results, with Davies et al. and prior X-state work explicitly credited.
3. **Decision consequence:** use identical hardware and candidate controls to measure the true rate lost by a policy optimized under a reduced state/noise model. The preliminary evidence includes 59.3% two-link and 54.2% four-link rate loss from a fidelity-matched Werner policy, a separate 13.8% three-link cutoff loss, and a 4.94% overestimate of the memory lifetime needed for key when the local amplitude-damping channel is Pauli-twirled. Show the zero-regret controls and regimes too; the homogeneous channel-twirl scans mostly selected the correct policy away from the key threshold.

Suggested abstract skeleton: *We develop a deterministic repeater-chain simulator that propagates time-resolved six-coordinate real-X states under local Pauli noise, amplitude damping, swapping, recurrence distillation, and memory cutoffs. A factorization of the success-weighted maps retains quasilinear evaluation in the time horizon. We establish when Bell-diagonal reduction preserves the relevant output and identify how non-unital memory evolution and outcome selection invalidate that reduction. Optimizing repeater actions with fidelity-only or Pauli-twirled models can select policies with substantially lower physical secret-key rate; in finite enumerated examples, a Werner-selected distillation policy loses over half the achievable pooled BB84 rate, while channel twirling shifts the predicted memory threshold for key. These findings motivate representation-aware control for repeater networks.* This is a proposal, not submission-ready prose; numerical scope and novelty must be checked against the full literature.

**Necessary before a journal submission:** (i) add at least one basis-adapted or alternative distillation comparator, or explicitly motivate why the hardware has only the implemented recurrence; (ii) map the decision loss over a systematic fidelity/memory/noise grid, including zero-loss regions and tie handling; (iii) include at least one hardware-anchored low-generation-probability scenario with slot duration and memory lifetimes in physical units; (iv) validate representative dense-state and direct-time-pair calculations and report truncation/FFT error; (v) state pooled asymptotic BB84, immediate Pauli-correction convention, finite candidate sets, destructive retry, and omitted finite-key/gate-noise effects plainly. A zero physical rate makes percentage regret undefined. Reporting only relative gains near zero would overstate practical importance.
